Project information

About Incunabula in Motion

Exploring the histories of individual copies

Incunabula in Motion is an experimental data visualisation by Stefan Matter. This version explores 3,394 copy records from 21 Swiss holding institutions, retrieved from Material Evidence in Incunabula (MEI) on 22 October 2026. It adapts the interface developed for Prayer Books in Motion. The library filter allows individual collections to be explored separately or together. These are the records currently identified as Swiss holdings in MEI, not a census of all incunabula held in Switzerland or the complete holdings of each library.

Sources and scope

MEI supplies copy identities, holding information and provenance. Edition references link to ISTC and the GW. Printing statements and language information use the October 2026 GW export supplied by Oliver Duntze. Multiple GW matches remain documented; no arbitrary candidate is selected.

This is a data-reuse experiment, not a new catalogue or a complete reconstruction of journeys. One map record represents one MEI copy record. Provenance is not automatically transferred between prints now bound together. Coverage reflects the available records, and missing evidence does not establish absence.

Filters and selection

The subject filter combines the bibliographic subject and keyword fields embedded in MEI. These describe editions, not individual ownership events. Source terms are retained, including differences in spelling and capitalisation. Choices within each filter are combined with OR; libraries, subjects, printing places, languages and text search are combined with AND. Multilingual editions match each language.

Use Add checked matches to collect copies across several searches, then Show selection to explore them together. This selection lasts while the page is open and is lost on reload.

Timeline and routes

The sequence begins with the printing place where identifiable and ends with the present holding institution. The last identifiable location is carried forward between dated records; this does not establish continuous presence. Unknown and conflicting dates remain visible in the full present-day sequence. GW normalised printing years serve as display anchors; approximate, before/after and other qualified dating remains flagged and the original date statement is displayed.

Lines are schematic. Dashed lines signal gaps or uncertainty; solid lines indicate adjacent dated records with no apparent gap. Neither proves an actual travel route. Clustering groups nearby places around frequently recorded locations, not historical territories. Wider lines represent more copies.

Locations and uncertainties

Circle area represents the number of copies at the selected year's last identifiable locations. The heatmap uses an approximate 200 km radius and a colour reference fixed for the selection across years and zoom levels. Regions are excluded from local map points. Coordinates represent localities, not library buildings.

Source values are retained separately from display decisions. Unidentified Seefeld and conflicting Zürich coordinates are withheld from the map pending clarification. Multiple or alternative printing places are not converted into invented sequential journeys. Consecutive records at the same point are visually consolidated, retaining their underlying evidence.

Credits and data preparation

ZB postincunabula: MARC test collection

The optional collection contains 31 selected catalogue references to ZB Zürich prints of 1501–1525, from a working corpus of 5,149 references exported through swisscollections on 23 October 2026. This is a narrow test, not the total number of copies with provenance. Each route shows the catalogue publication locality, one selected dated institutional event and the current holding. References may describe bound-with prints or multiple volumes.

Two event dates are circa 1540 and one is probably 1832. The slider uses these years as approximate anchors; the details retain their qualifications. Current holding is an observation, not an acquisition date. MEI subject filters exclude MARC references. Reset restores the MEI-only view; saved selections retain explicitly added copies.

Thanks to the cataloguers and institutions contributing to MEI, ISTC and GW; to Falk Eisermann and Oliver Duntze for advice and the GW export; and to CERL and Helena Nebel for enabling scripted retrieval. Coordinates are supplemented by GeoNames (CC BY 4.0). The map uses Leaflet and OpenStreetMap contributors' data.
